How to play audio through two outputs at once on a Mac

macOS plays everything on one output. Getting sound out of two takes a workaround, and each one has a cost.

What macOS gives you.

  1. One output at a time

    System Settings > Sound > Output lets you pick one device, and every app follows it.

  2. A Multi-Output Device

    Audio MIDI Setup can join several outputs into one device, for every app at once.

  3. No choice for each app

    macOS can’t play music on the speakers and a call on the headphones.

What a Multi-Output Device costs you.

Audio MIDI Setup’s Multi-Output Device plays the same sound on every output in it. It has no volume control of its own, so the volume keys stop working while it’s selected, and every app goes to all of its outputs.

Outputs also play at slightly different delays. Two wired devices in the same room can sound like an echo, and Bluetooth adds more delay than a cable.

Common questions.

Can a Mac play sound on two devices at once?

Yes, with a Multi-Output Device in Audio MIDI Setup or with an app. macOS on its own plays on one output.

Why don’t my volume keys work with a Multi-Output Device?

A Multi-Output Device has no volume control of its own, so macOS has nothing to change. Set each device’s level in Audio MIDI Setup instead.

Can I send different apps to different speakers?

Not with macOS alone. Decibar Pro lets you pick an output for each app, or several outputs for one app.

Why does it sound like an echo?

Each device plays with its own delay. Decibar lines up wired devices, and lets you set a delay by hand for Bluetooth and AirPlay, where the reported delay is less accurate.

Does it work with AirPlay?

AirPlay and Bluetooth add a delay of their own. If one still sounds late, raise its delay in Decibar’s Sound settings.
