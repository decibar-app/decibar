Check that this is your problem.

  1. Find the output

    Open System Settings > Sound > Output and select your monitor.

  2. Look at the slider

    If the output volume slider is disabled, macOS has nothing to change on this monitor.

  3. Press a volume key

    A “not available” symbol on screen confirms it. The monitor’s speakers keep playing at the level set on the monitor.

Where the level lives.

Built-in speakers and USB audio devices offer macOS a volume control. HDMI and DisplayPort outputs don’t. The signal is digital, and the amp inside the monitor sets how loud it plays.

That leaves three ways to change the loudness: tell the monitor, lower the sound before it reaches the monitor, or use a different output. A combined device from Audio MIDI Setup doesn’t help, because it gets no overall volume either.

Common questions.

Why is the volume slider greyed out for my monitor?

HDMI and DisplayPort carry digital audio straight to the monitor, and the amp inside the monitor sets the loudness. macOS sees no volume control on that output, so the slider in Sound settings is disabled and the volume keys show a “not available” symbol.

Is this a bug in macOS?

No. It’s how these outputs behave on a Mac. Restarting or reinstalling won’t add a level, because the device offers macOS none to change.

Does DisplayPort or USB-C behave differently from HDMI?

DisplayPort behaves the same way. A USB-C cable depends on what it carries: DisplayPort over USB-C behaves like DisplayPort, while a monitor that appears as a USB audio device can offer macOS a normal volume control.

Can I go quieter than the monitor’s lowest setting?

Not with the monitor’s own volume. Lowering the sound before it leaves the Mac can, because it doesn’t depend on the amp. Decibar’s software volume goes down to −40 dB.

Do I need DDC/CI?

Only to change the monitor’s own volume. Software volume works without it, so a monitor, dock or adapter that blocks DDC/CI can still get a level.
